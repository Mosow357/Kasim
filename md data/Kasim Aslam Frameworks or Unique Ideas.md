## 1. 3X Freedom

**[Original — his stated life mission]**

Kasim's stated goal is "to help as many people as I possibly can achieve what I call 3X Freedom."

| Freedom | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Financial freedom** | Your investments pay for your whole lifestyle, so you never have to work for money again. |
| **Time & location freedom** | You control your calendar and where you live. "I can cancel any meeting on my calendar." |
| **Freedom of purpose** | You spend your life on what you're meant to do. For him that means teaching, "executing on my purpose." |

- **The precondition:** "In order to achieve 3X freedom, we have to change the way we think about money."
- **The payoff is legacy.** When you're financially free, "guess what else is free? Your children."
- **Brand asset:** There is a free "3X Freedom" community. The CTA on the promo page is "JOIN 3X FREEDOM FOR FREE."

## 2. The Machine Model of Economic Life

**[Original — his core money paradigm, drawn on a whiteboard]**

### 2.1 Core premise: think of your economic life as a system of machines

- Every machine is an **input → output** mechanism: metal becomes sprockets, data becomes information, meat becomes sausage.
- **Why machines?** Thinking about people triggers empathy, and empathy makes decisions hard. Thinking in machines lets you "think logically, and then we can weave empathy" back in.

### 2.2 The Time-for-Money Machine

- This is the first machine most people meet in adult life: **time in → money out**. Example: $20/hour × 8 hours = $160/day.
- **The ceiling:** You don't have 24 hours. About 8 go to sleep and about 8 to work. The 8 that are left are for errands, fun, family and *purpose*. The most you can do is double your working time, and then you burn out.
- **Contrarian nuance:** He rejects the "armchair financial thought leaders" who say *never* trade time for money. It's the right trade in the beginning because time is "all we had to begin with." Most people who think they've escaped it haven't. His real-estate friend still trades time for money; he has just "moved the fulcrum."
- **Litmus test for business owners:** "Can you walk away from your business for an indefinite period of time… without the business atrophying? If not, you're trading time for money."

### 2.3 The Fulcrum (Leverage)

- Every machine sits on a fulcrum. **Moving the fulcrum** means getting more money out of the same time.
- **How to move it:**
  - Make your time more valuable through skilled labor, on-the-job growth or promotions.
  - **Start a business.** This is the best lever: "Being a business owner instantly moves the fulcrum." A chef earns less than a restaurant owner.
- **Warning:** Starting a business *increases* your hours at first. "You're going to be the worst boss you've ever had in your entire life… something of a slave driver."
- **Start the business to sell it.** Build an *asset*, not monthly income. He cites Perry Belcher's research: about **68%** of the money high-net-worth people put into real estate came from **business exits**, about 20–25% from inheritance, and the rest from windfalls. **[Borrowed data]**
- **"You don't own a business, you own a job"** until the business runs without you.

### 2.4 The YOU Machine

**[Original acronym]**

This is where most people **burn** the money they earn.

| Letter | Stands for | Plain meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **Y** | Yearnings | Wants |
| **O** | Obligations | Needs |
| **U** | Unearned | Debt (spending money you haven't earned yet) |

- **The trap:** Most people put 100% of their earnings into the YOU Machine, and some put in 101% or more. As income rises, the YOU Machine grows with it: bigger house, nicer car, more debt. That forces more time back into the Time-for-Money Machine.
- **Who benefits:** "This is the trap that everybody wants you in… because it makes you a good consumer." He says it isn't a conspiracy. The economy is a flywheel that gets stronger the more people depend on it. Coworkers, bosses, bankers and even family prefer you *predictable*.
- **Expenses follow expenses:** His BMW example. The lease is only $100/month more than a Civic, but premium gas, double insurance, pricey tires and oil changes, covered parking and dent anxiety all follow.

### 2.5 Money Is Fuel

**[Original — "if you take nothing else away…"]**

- "Money is fuel." Either you burn it, in the YOU Machine, or you invest it in machines that make more fuel.
- **Savings is garbage.** "It's money under the mattress. All it does is atrophy." Inflation eats it, and saving doesn't get you out of the trap. Retirement accounts push your money "into the future so you continue to be a good economic participant."
- **Invest in things that yield.** That means low-risk vehicles too, like government bonds. Don't lock money up until you're 65.
- **Money gives you options.** His story: a business valued at $10M was offered for $1M on 24 hours' notice, and he was one of the few in his high-net-worth circle who had the cash. Most were "asset rich and cash poor."

### 2.6 Investment Machines

- These are machines where **money goes in and more money comes out**: other businesses, real estate (single-family homes, syndications), hard-money loans, or your own business's yielding assets.
- **Freedom defined:** Your investments fully pay for your YOU Machine, so you can turn off the Time-for-Money Machine. You become "**self-contained**."
- **The Investment Waterfall:** money → investments → their returns → more investments → and so on. He says he has "five, going on six, six-figure incomes" from separate investments.

### 2.7 The Golden Rule of Money

> "Money that you buy with your time can't be spent on anything other than absolute necessities, and then machines that make you more money. The money that the machines make you — you spend on whatever you want."

That second pot can buy "a BMW for every day of the week in a different color." He himself still lives "two standard deviations under my tax bracket" and drives a decades-old Honda.

### 2.8 The Three Steps

1. **Downsize the YOU Machine.** Kill debt "faster than you've ever done in the history of history," starting with the highest interest rate. Downsize your home, car, habits and travel.
2. **Increase the value of your time.** Move the fulcrum with skills, or better, a business.
3. **Invest, don't save.**

### 2.9 Supporting principles

- **Non-negotiables:** He won't compromise on **safety and health**. His "shoebox condo" was in a very safe neighborhood with good schools, and his family buys organic food.
- **One illogical splurge is allowed.** His was guns. "That's not what's killing you. What's killing you is the way you think about money."
- **Radical frugality with proof:** He was a liquid millionaire living in the cheapest-per-square-foot condo in his zip code, sharing one car, with a newborn. His honeymoon in Ireland cost $1,500 all-in.
- **Slavery reframe:** Slavery is "work performed under threat of punishment or coercion." If you stop working, you lose everything, so "that's slavery. Are these expenditures worth being enslaved? … The answer is no."
- **Perspective:** "The poorest people in Western society have a higher quality of life than the wealthiest people in the 1800s."
- **Disneyland trade-off:** Would your kids rather go to Disneyland every year, or have you "100% financially free" five years from now?

## 3. Do the Things That Don't Scale (Post-AI Growth)

**[Original framing of a known idea]**

### 3.1 Scale Is a Paved Road

- Your business is a vehicle off-roading through a jungle. Scale means finding the dirt road that becomes a paved road, and then "you're off, 100 miles an hour."
- **The post-AI twist:** "All paved roads, AI can traverse. If it scales, AI can do it."
- **So the entrepreneurial unlock is the non-scalable work:** direct mail, handwritten thank-you notes, calling clients to thank them, event booths, speaking, podcasting, "knocking on doors, shaking hands, kissing babies."

### 3.2 AI Slop Burns Channels

- LinkedIn outreach *used to* work when it was well researched. Then AI automated it "from soup to nuts," and now the inbox is "all AI slop."
- Marketers "took the thing that used to be really important… and they ruined the damn thing." He admits he is one of those "evil marketers."

### 3.3 Find the Surrogate: What Itch Was the Channel Scratching?

- When a channel dies, ask what *need* it served. LinkedIn was "the most efficient way to allow business people to connect over shared interests."
- Then find the **non-scalable** way to meet that same need.

### 3.4 People Go Tribal

- "Anytime there's a massive shift or an extinction event, folks get real tribal real quick… it's a mechanism of safety."
- AI is that kind of shift. Find the tribe in your industry: events, Zoom calls, forums, masterminds.

### 3.5 Give First, Give Last, Give More

**[Signature mantra]**

> "Be the first one to give. Just show up giving. Then give more than anybody would expect… and make sure that you're always the last one to give."

- This "evokes the law of reciprocity in a way that will blow your mind and change your life."
- **Be in the room.** The room is a metaphor, and it can be digital.
- **If there's no room, build one.** Launch a mastermind or group. His example is a daily open Zoom at 3:00 p.m. Pacific, streamed live, that anyone could join. The name is garbled in the transcript as "the Daily Sai." People came every day. It was so powerful "it wore me out," and it didn't scale.

### 3.6 Dream 100

**[Borrowed — "It's definitely not mine"]**

- This is a list of the 100 clients who could change the face of your business. Landing even one or two should be transformational. "If the Dream 100 isn't robust enough, you're not thinking big enough."

**Kasim's extension: the customer that feeds you customers.** His septic-company example:

| Level | Target | Value |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | One homeowner | One pump |
| 2 | An HOA on a shared lift station | ~300 contracts |
| 3 | A manufacturing facility with an industrial lift station | A big account |
| 4 | **The property-management firms that run those facilities** | A customer who *feeds you more customers* |

"You can do this in every industry… a food truck, a dentist, a traveling vacuum salesman."

### 3.7 The Community Partnership Play

This is Pareto Talent's Dream 100 strategy:

- Everyone with a community is an educator, "whether or not they want to admit it."
- **Offer:** Pareto trains its AI-enabled right hands on everything a specific community needs: its jargon, software, deadlines ("why April's do-or-die time" for CFOs) and the leader's paradigm and teachings.
- **Deal:** The community leader refers members and gets a **profit share for the life of the relationship**. It costs them nothing, helps their members execute, improves retention and makes the leader look great.
- **Delivery:** A physical, customized direct-mail sales letter that Kasim stuffed and mailed himself, on camera.

### 3.8 Content While Doing

- "When you create content while you're doing things, it just makes people interested."
- **Rule for low-leverage work:** If there's "a better-than-zero chance of potentially 10x-ing my business," doing work below your pay grade is still worth it.

## 4. Delegation & Talent Frameworks

Source: an interview with his first executive assistant, whose name is transcribed as Ivan, Ivon or Avon. That assistant grew into his CTO and partner, and now co-owns three businesses with him, one with an eight-figure valuation.

### 4.1 The Firefighter Trap

- In the early years, "every single day I'd wake up to fires." Kasim thought "that's what business was" and treated firefighting as a badge of honor, to the point of setting fires in order to put them out. "You're doing this to yourself."
- **Limiting beliefs he names:**
  - "If you want something done, do it yourself."
  - "There's no such thing as good help."
  - Fear that a competent hire will steal clients.
  - Needing to know how everything is done.

### 4.2 The Cost of Cheap

**[Signature line]**

> "If you think a high-paid employee is expensive, wait till you see what a cheap employee costs you."

- He tried bargain freelancers first (Upwork, oDesk). Every hire "would turn into their own unique type of fire."
- **Turning point:** He hired John Moran, a demanding former client, at a salary equal to Kasim's own. Within 90 days the business was turning around.
- **Top-talent thesis:** He went international so he could "pay the most," not the least, relative to the local market. He paid Ivan **20% more than he asked for** and held him to "the absolute highest standard."

### 4.3 Paint Their Picture, Their Way

- The people he trained did poorly. The stars came in with skill or figured it out themselves.
- "You don't want them painting your picture your way. You want them painting their picture their way."
- **His mistake:** Looking for "somebody who could do it good enough my way" instead of "better their way."

### 4.4 The 10/10 vs. 11/10 Employee

**[Original]**

- **10/10:** Give them 10 things, they do all 10, and you never follow up. A 9/10 feels like an A student but is "effectively worthless," because you still have to watch all 10. "I'm paying you to worry."
- **11/10:** They do the 10, plus the one thing you forgot or didn't think of. This is where "an employee becomes a partner."
- **Push back with reasons.** The cupcake story: "You don't want a three-tier cake. You want 10 cupcakes. Let me tell you why."
- **Speed plus accountability:** Instant acknowledgment ("Okay," "Got it") builds confidence. Speed on the front end has to be matched by accountability on the back end.
- **Retain them:** When you feel "I can't lose this person," your only job is to "build a situation that's better than their alternative." Ask often: "How are you doing? Is there anything I can do better?"

### 4.5 Delegate Outcomes, Not Tasks (Projects, Not Tasks)

**[Borrowed mantra, put into practice his own way]**

- Task assignment is "how most managers manage, and it's devastatingly inefficient." It is "a byproduct of bureaucracy… middle management and micromanaging." He calls ClickUp, Asana "and all that garbage" tools built for it.
- It only works once you have people you trust.
- **Great talent bristles at tasks.** They ask "why? what for?" about the audience, goal, offer and target CPA, because the context changes the result.
- **Builders find the critical line.** Planners at a whiteboard add endless boxes to the flowchart because they won't have to build them. The person doing the work cuts to what's necessary and builds on it later. A "pragmatic strategist."

### 4.6 The Black Box of Delegation

**[Original — named but only briefly explained in the sources]**

- Hand over the *end result*, not the steps inside. It grew out of working with Ivan and a later assistant, Julianne.
- ⚠️ *Gap:* The full framework isn't laid out in these transcripts. Find a primary source before building on it.

### 4.7 When to Stop Checking the Work

- You can let go when **their standards are higher than yours.** "I was like, it's good, send it. And you were like, no." At that point "it's no longer even about trust."

### 4.8 The 2-6-2 Framework

**[Original — "this was just me figuring out"]**

- Give someone 10 different responsibilities. They'll be **bad at 2**, **good at 6** ("check the box, well done") and **world-class at 2**, "better than any human you've ever encountered."
- **Don't coach the bottom 2.** Even if coaching helps, the gain isn't worth the time, effort and energy. Take those tasks off their plate.
- Borrowed quote, which he admits is misattributed to Einstein: "If you judge a fish by its ability to climb a tree, it spends its whole life thinking it's stupid."
- **Adele, Jordan & Banksy:** "Adele should sing, Jordan should play basketball, Banksy should paint… The minute Adele is not singing, you have failed as a leader."
- **The leader as coach, not HR.** He says he hates HR because it's reactive. The job is to find what people "can't not do" and then "get everything else out of their way." He likens it to a sports coach, and to protecting star players from team members who drain them.

### 4.9 Delegate Messy

**[Original — his "do this this week" action]**

- Send every person on your org chart a **two-minute voice message** (WhatsApp, SMS, Slack) with the *end result* of a project you want them to own.
- His example: "I'd love to start a faceless YouTube channel… do the research, analyze the competition, tell me what we should shoot content on, and build me the 90-day roadmap."
- Use it for the things that "feel too big to delegate": a new product, market, avatar, partners, or a book. "Every founder should write a book."
- Ten people take 20 minutes. "Spend the weekend sending those messages. You'll never see your business move faster."
- **Talent surfaces:** "The people that are really capable surface right away. And the people that aren't… that's a good thing to know, too."

### 4.10 "Right Hands," Not "Assistants"

- "I hate that word. An assistant gets your coffee and checks your emails. We call them right hands."
- Pareto Talent's right hands are **AI-enabled**, and "it takes a thousand applicants to produce one."
- **Why it matters:** "If you give a founder time," they can do podcasts, partnerships, events and speaking, "and then things just start to take off."

## 5. Contrarian Positions (Quick Reference)

| Conventional wisdom | Kasim's position |
|---|---|
| Never trade time for money | It's the right trade early on. Almost everyone still does it. |
| Save for retirement | "Savings is garbage." Invest in things that yield *now*. |
| Get a degree | Hasn't been "economically valuable for the last 20 years" (maybe doctor, lawyer, accountant). |
| Real estate makes millionaires | Most of that money came from *selling businesses* first. |
| Being your own boss is freedom | You'll be the worst boss you've ever had, at first. |
| Hire cheap to save money | Cheap is the most expensive hire. Pay above the ask. |
| Manage with tasks and PM tools | Delegate outcomes. Task management is bureaucracy. |
| Coach employees on weaknesses | Delete weaknesses and amplify the top 2 (2-6-2). |
| Only do what scales | In a post-AI world, what scales is commoditized. Do what doesn't. |
| Automate outreach with AI | That creates slop and burns channels. Go tribal, human and physical. |
| 90% is an A | A 9/10 employee is "effectively worthless." |
