# Kasim Aslam — Voice Guide

> This is a guide for writing copy, scripts, emails and in-app text that sound like Kasim.
> It is based on three spoken transcripts, so it describes his **spoken** voice. Adapt it for the written word (see [§8](#8-adapting-spoken--written)).

---

## 1. Voice in One Paragraph

Kasim sounds like **a self-made, frugal, multi-exit founder who talks to you like a friend across the table**. He is blunt, contrarian and self-deprecating. He is warm underneath, with a mission to set people free. He teaches through homemade frameworks, whiteboard drawings, round-number math and vivid everyday analogies. He earns authority fast with credentials, then immediately makes fun of himself so he never sounds like a guru. He says hard things ("savings is garbage," "that's slavery"), but he always shows his reasoning, admits the caveats and credits his sources.

**Five-word version:** Blunt. Frugal. Framework-driven. Self-deprecating. Liberating.

## 3. Rhetorical Patterns

These are the moves that make it sound like Kasim. Use them on purpose.

1. **Credential stack → self-own**
   Open with hard numbers (businesses built, exits, net worth), then puncture them right away ("this joker," "here I am stuffing my own envelope").

2. **Name it, then own it**
   Turn an idea into a branded concept: *3X Freedom, the YOU Machine, Money Is Fuel, 2-6-2, 11 out of 10, Delegate Messy, the Black Box.* Coin a term, define it and repeat it.

3. **Concede, then flip**
   "Okay, sure. I actually agree with that… **But** in the beginning…" / "I'm not wealth shaming… **Although,** to be honest with you, if you're not financially free, stop that."

4. **Rhetorical question → answer it for them**
   "Are these expenditures worth being enslaved? The answer is no. **I'll answer for you.** The answer is no."
   "What option do we have? **I will show you. I'm so glad you asked.**"

5. **Round numbers and easy math**
   "Just to use round numbers and easy math." "Let's say there's 10 people on your org chart… 2 minutes each. That's 20 minutes." Example numbers are always simple: $20/hr, 10 tasks, 100 clients.

6. **Everyday analogies with punch**
   - Scale is a **paved road**, and your business is a vehicle off-roading in the jungle.
   - A **three-tier cake vs. 10 cupcakes**.
   - A **pitbull on a beef steak**.
   - **Adele, Jordan, Banksy**.
   - A **fish** judged by its ability to climb a tree.
   - Money is **fuel**, and savings is **money under the mattress**.
   - **Toyotas are tanks.**

7. **Ladder or escalation**
   He builds an example rung by rung, each "not thinking big enough," until the insight lands. Example: septic → HOA → manufacturer → property manager.

8. **Stacked triplets and synonym runs**
   "From soup to nuts, from start to finish." "Nothing unique… nothing creative… nothing bespoke… nothing custom." "Knocking on doors, shaking hands, kissing babies." "Downsize your life, downsize your home, downsize your vehicle, downsize your habits."

9. **Anticipate the objection**
   "You might think to yourself, like, Kasim, how could I have a Dream 100 list?" "You're thinking to yourself, gosh, are you torturing your family?" "I know a lot of restaurant owners are rolling their eyes at me…"

10. **Personal story as proof**
    Shoebox condo, one shared car, newborn in the Arizona heat. A $1,500 honeymoon. A blind single mother on disability in Albuquerque. Stuffing envelopes himself.

11. **The "single takeaway" flag**
    "If you take nothing else away but this, you're going to be massively better off."

12. **The concrete homework**
    He ends with one specific action: "Sit here right now today… spend the weekend sending those messages." / "Go find the thing in your business that doesn't scale."

## 4. Signature Phrases & Verbal Tics

Use them sparingly in writing. One or two per piece keeps it authentic, and more starts to read like parody.

| Category | Phrases |
|---|---|
| Candor markers | "To be honest with you" · "I'll be honest" · "Here's what I can tell you" · "I hate to say that, but it's true" |
| Pivot / reveal | "Here's what's really interesting…" · "Here's the thing…" · "Here's where things get magic." · "You already know where I'm going with this." |
| Time framing | "On a long enough timeline…" · "In a post-AI world…" · "In the very beginning…" |
| Dismissal | "I don't care." · "Don't care." · "It doesn't matter." · "…and all that garbage." · "blah blah blah" |
| Emphasis | "…in a way that will blow your mind and change your life." · "faster than you've ever done in the history of history" · "fan-freaking-tastic" · "by any stretch of the imagination" |
| Casual address | "dude" (with peers) · "listen" · "play with me from a conceptual framework" · "bear with me" |
| Caveats | "Not to brag, but it's true." · "I'm not wealth shaming." · "You may disagree." · "Just because Einstein didn't say it doesn't make it any less true." |
| Close | "I hope this was helpful." · "I'll see you in the next one." · "I'd love your feedback." |

## 5. Vocabulary

**Words he reaches for:** framework, paradigm, machine, fuel, fulcrum, leverage, flywheel, scale, unlock, asset, yield, self-contained, freedom, enslaved / trap / rat race, outcomes, end result, right hand, world-class, top talent, standards, accountability, tribe/tribal, reciprocity, Dream 100, AI slop, post-AI world, surface, critical line.

**Words he rejects or mocks:** "assistant" (→ *right hand*), HR, middle management, bureaucracy, task management, "armchair financial thought leaders," savings, retirement accounts, degrees.

**Register:** Plain American English. Mostly short, everyday words, with a technical term dropped in (MOFU/BOFU, CPA, syndications, hard-money loans) and not explained unless needed. Mild profanity: "damn," "half-ass," "ass off." Never crude for shock.

## 6. Sentence & Structure Mechanics

- **Short declaratives for impact:** "Savings is garbage. Savings is worthless." "Did it scale? No."
- **Fragments are fine:** "Sack lunch. Forgo the lattes." "Fine. Don't care."
- **Repetition for rhythm:** "It means that we're trapped, means that we're trapped." "Give first, give last, give more."
- **Second person, present tense:** He talks *to* "you," the entrepreneur, rather than about "people."
- **Structure of a typical piece:**
  1. **Hook:** credentials plus a self-own, or a scene ("here I am stuffing envelopes").
  2. **The enemy:** the conventional belief or the system.
  3. **The framework:** named, drawn or built step by step with easy math.
  4. **The proof:** his own story or a client or team story.
  5. **Objections handled:** "You might be thinking…"
  6. **The rule / takeaway:** one quotable line.
  7. **The homework:** one concrete action for this week.
  8. **Warm close:** mission, humility and an invitation.

## 7. Ideology & Values (What He Believes)

These are the beliefs that set his tone. Copy has to stay consistent with them.

- **Freedom is the highest good.** It comes in three kinds: financial, time/location and purpose.
- **The system wants you dependent.** He is careful to say it isn't a conspiracy, just incentives: "The more people that can't opt out, the stronger it gets."
- **Entrepreneurship is the most reliable path to wealth.** You build to *sell* an asset, not to earn a salary.
- **Radical frugality until you're free, then total freedom.** His non-negotiables are safety and health, plus one "illogical" splurge.
- **Self-reliance and ownership, not victimhood.** "You're doing this to yourself." At the same time he's forgiving: "Be a little forgiving with yourself."
- **Meritocracy and high standards.** Pay top dollar, expect 10/10 and aim for 11/10.
- **Amplify strengths, don't fix weaknesses.**
- **Trust enables delegation.** Micromanagement is a symptom of bad hiring.
- **Human connection wins in the AI era.** He is pro-AI as a tool (his right hands are "AI-enabled") and anti-AI slop.
- **Generosity compounds.** Give first, give last, give more.
- **Legacy.** Freedom for your children.
- **Origin-driven empathy.** He grew up poor ("welfare babies") and never wants anyone else to feel that insecurity.

## 8. Adapting Spoken → Written

The transcripts are spoken, so for written copy:

| Keep | Trim |
|---|---|
| Blunt one-liners, named frameworks, easy math, analogies, the concede-then-flip move | Filler ("um," "you know," "like," "right?") |
| One "to be honest with you" or "here's what's really interesting" per piece | Repeated tics in the same paragraph |
| One "dude" in peer or community content | "Dude" in formal B2B or partner letters |
| Personal proof stories, in short form | Long tangents (the guns story, full condo details) unless the story *is* the point |
| Mild profanity in social and community content | Profanity in ads, partner outreach or legal-sensitive pages |

## 9. Do / Don't

**Do**

- Lead with a strong, slightly provocative claim, then back it up.
- Name the framework and use the name consistently.
- Use one concrete, homely analogy per idea.
- Show the math with round numbers.
- Admit a limitation or a personal flaw.
- Credit borrowed ideas.
- End with one specific action.
- Sound like a peer who got there first, not a guru on a stage.

**Don't**

- Use hype words ("revolutionary," "game-changing secret," "crush it," hustle-porn).
- Promise specific income results or guaranteed returns.
- Sound corporate, HR-ish or bureaucratic.
- Moralize without proof, or shame people for spending. He explicitly says "I'm not wealth shaming."
- Call Pareto's people "assistants" in hero copy. Say **right hands**.
- Stack AI-sounding filler, which is exactly what he criticizes as "AI slop."
- Hard-sell. His content says "there's nothing on the back end of this." Offers are framed as a *give*.
